Taipei

All about Taiwan's capital

Taipei is a busy, friendly city tucked between green mountains. Tall towers, old temples, and yummy night markets are all a quick subway ride apart.

🏙️

Taipei 101

Bamboo skyscraper

Once the world's tallest building at 508 metres! Shaped like stacked bamboo for good luck. A giant gold ball inside swings to keep it steady during typhoons.

🏮

Shilin Night Market

Snack heaven

Taipei's biggest night market — hundreds of glowing stalls selling popcorn chicken, bubble tea, fun games, and plenty of treats to share.

🏯

National Palace Museum

Treasure house

Home to nearly 700,000 ancient Chinese treasures. Look for the famous jade cabbage and the rock that looks just like a piece of meat!

🐉

Longshan Temple

Old, beautiful temple

Built way back in 1738. Red lanterns glow, dragons curl on the rooftops, and people clap and bow to make wishes.

🚇

Taipei MRT

Easy, clean metro

The subway is fast, quiet and friendly for kids. Tap on with an EasyCard to zip across the city — but no eating or drinking on the train!

Taiwan's Animals

Meet Taiwan's wild residents

Taiwan has lots of unique creatures that live nowhere else on Earth. Here are five of the most beloved.

Taiwan Blue Magpie

Bright blue beauty

Taiwan's national bird, found only here. Brilliant blue body, black head, red beak, and a super-long tail twice the length of its body. Loud, smart, and very protective of their nests!

Formosan Black Bear

Symbol of Taiwan

Taiwan's only native bear, with a bright white "V" on its chest. Shy and rare — they live high in the mountain forests, and seeing one is very lucky.

Taiwan Beauty Snake

Pretty patterns

A long, gentle, non-venomous snake with a black stripe across its eyes and beautiful patterned scales. They glide through forests and farms, helping out by eating mice.

Formosan Rock Macaque

Taiwan's monkey

Found only in Taiwan! These clever mountain monkeys live in big family troops in the forests, with grey-brown fur and pink faces. Watch out — they're known to swipe snacks from hikers!

Alishan Salamander

A living fossil

Tiny, slippery, and very rare — these little salamanders live only in Taiwan's cold, high-mountain streams. Their family has been around since the Ice Age!

Taiwan Birds

Feathered friends of the island

Taiwan is a paradise for birdwatchers — more than 600 bird species call the island home, and over two dozen live nowhere else on Earth. Here are some of the most loved.

Taiwan Blue Magpie

台灣藍鵲 · Endemic

Called the "Long-tailed Mountain Lady," it's Taiwan's national bird. Bright blue with a black head, red beak, and a tail almost as long as its body.

Mikado Pheasant

帝雉 · "King of the Mist"

This shy, midnight-blue pheasant lives high in the misty mountains. It's so beloved that it appears on Taiwan's NT$1,000 bill!

Swinhoe's Pheasant

藍腹鷴 · Endemic

A glittering blue-bellied pheasant with red legs, a white crest, and a snowy back. It walks through Taiwan's forests like royalty.

Taiwan Barbet

五色鳥 · "Five-Colored Bird"

A chubby little rainbow! Green body, yellow forehead, red throat, blue cheeks. Its "tok-tok-tok" call sounds like a monk tapping a wooden block.

Black-faced Spoonbill

黑面琵鷺 · Winter visitor

Every winter, thousands of these elegant white birds with spoon-shaped bills fly to Taiwan's wetlands. They're rare and very loved.

Formosan Whistling Thrush

台灣紫嘯鶇 · Endemic

Lives near mountain waterfalls. Its loud, high whistle sounds like a squeaky bike — once you've heard it, you can't forget it!

Crested Serpent Eagle

大冠鷲 · Mountain hunter

You'll often hear its lonely "hyu-hyu-hyu" cry from high above the forest. It hunts snakes and lizards — and yes, it has a punky little crest.

Taiwan Yuhina

冠羽畫眉 · Endemic

A tiny, social songbird with a spiky brown mohawk. They travel in cheerful chatty flocks through the mountain forests.

Malayan Night Heron

黑冠麻鷺 · "Park Big Uncle"

The slow, stocky bird that stands very still on park lawns hunting for worms. People call it the "Park Big Uncle" because it loves city parks and isn't afraid of anyone — kids walk right past it!

Black-collared Starling

黑領椋鳥

A handsome white bird with a smart black "collar" around its chest. Loud and chatty, often seen in noisy little gangs in fields and parks.

Light-vented Bulbul

白頭翁 · "White-headed Grandpa"

Look for the snowy white patch on the back of its head! Found everywhere — gardens, parks, balconies — singing cheerfully from sunrise.

Eurasian Tree Sparrow

麻雀 · The town hopper

Taiwan's everywhere-bird! Recognise it by the chocolate-brown cap and tiny black cheek spot. Often hops around looking for fallen rice grains.

Black-naped Oriole

黃鸝 · Sunshine bird

A flash of pure golden yellow with a black eye-mask, like a tiny banana with sunglasses. Sings a flute-like "wee-loo!" from treetops.

White-eared Sibia

白耳畫眉 · Endemic

A medium mountain bird with a long, bold white stripe sweeping back from its eye like a feathery moustache. Hangs out in cheerful little flocks.

White-whiskered Laughingthrush

金翼白眉 · Endemic

The friendly bandit of the high mountains — brown body, glowing golden wings, white "eyebrows" and "whiskers". Often hops up to greet hikers.

Japanese White-eye

綠繡眼 · "Embroidered Eye"

A tiny olive-green ball of energy with a perfectly round white ring around each eye. Loves nectar and hangs upside down on blossoms.

Oriental Magpie-Robin

鵲鴝

A neat little black-and-white singer with a long upright tail. Males perch on fence-posts and sing loud, musical songs at dawn.

Spot-billed Duck

花嘴鴨 · Pond paddler

A brown duck with a dark beak that has a bright yellow tip — like it dipped its bill in mustard! Loves rice paddies, lakes and city ponds.

Little Egret

小白鷺 · Wetland dancer

A slim, snow-white bird with a thin black bill, black legs and bright yellow "shoes". Stalks fish slowly in rice paddies and rivers.

Myna

八哥

A clever, glossy black bird with a yellow eye-patch and yellow bill. Mynas are famous mimics — some can copy human words and even doorbells!

Spotted Dove

珠頸斑鳩 · "Pearl-necked Dove"

A soft, gentle dove with a pretty "necklace" of white spots on black across its neck. Coos lullabies in parks and gardens all day long.

Collared Scops Owl

領角鴞 · The night watcher

A small, fluffy owl with little ear-tufts and big golden eyes. Lives in city parks too — listen for its soft "boop... boop..." call at night.

Scarlet Minivet

赤紅山椒鳥

Males look like a flying flame — bright red with jet-black wings and head. Females wear cheerful yellow. They flit through the treetops in pairs.

Gray Treepie

灰樹鵲

A relative of the Blue Magpie — slate-grey body, black head and a long sweeping tail. Bold, loud, and often seen flying low through forest edges.

Black Kite

黑鳶 · Sky soarer

A big dark brown raptor with a forked tail. You can spot them gliding in lazy circles above the coast and over harbours — they're skilled scavengers.

Pokémon

Pokémon 🎮

Mezastar

Mezastar is a super-fun Pokémon arcade game that arrived in Taiwan on April 2, 2025! Battle wild Pokémon on a big screen and win real star-shaped collectable tags that pop out of the machine.

🏪

Pokémon Center Taipei

Taiwan has one of only TWO official Pokémon Centers outside Japan in the whole world! Find it on the 3rd floor of Shin Kong Mitsukoshi A11, right next to Taipei 101.

⭐ Mezastar

⭐ The Star-Shaped Tags

Every game you get a Meza Tag — a shiny star-shaped tile with a Pokémon on it. Common tags have red stars, rare ones have yellow stars, and the rarest have rainbow sparkly holographic designs! Legendary Pokémon like Mewtwo and Zacian are the hardest to get.

🎯 How to Play

Put a coin in, then place your star tags on the glowing lanes on the panel. Press the buttons to power up your Pokémon's moves and attack! Win 3 rounds and you catch the wild Pokémon — and earn a brand new tag from the machine!

📍 Where to Find it in Taiwan

Look for Mezastar machines at Mezastar Station Taipei (near Taipei Main Station), inside Sogo and Shin Kong Mitsukoshi department stores, and at Pokémon Center Taipei where special limited tags are sometimes given out!

🤝 Team Up!

Each Mezastar machine has two stations side by side sharing one big screen. That means you and a friend (or a parent!) can battle together as a team. Two sets of tags means twice the power!

🌟 Fun Facts

The name "Mezastar" means aiming for the stars! Some special TAG Meza Tags show two Pokémon on one tile. The game is so popular it has spread to Japan, Hong Kong, Singapore, Malaysia, the Philippines, and South Korea too!

🏪 Pokémon Center Taipei

🌏 Only the 2nd in the World Outside Japan!

Pokémon Center Taipei opened on December 8, 2023 — it is only the second official Pokémon Center ever opened outside Japan in the entire world! The number 8 in Chinese culture is considered very lucky, so opening on the 8th was extra special.

📍 Where is it?

It's on the 3rd floor of Shin Kong Mitsukoshi A11, No. 11 Songshou Road, Xinyi District — right next to Taipei 101! To get there, take the MRT Blue Line to Taipei City Hall Station, Exit 2 and walk about 8 minutes.

🕐 Opening Hours

Mon–Thu & Sun: 11:00 AM – 9:30 PM
Fri & Sat: 11:00 AM – 10:00 PM
Best time to visit: right when it opens on a weekday — less crowded!

🛍️ What Can You Buy?

Plush toys, Pokémon Trading Cards, clothing, keychains, stationery, and mugs. Best of all: Taiwan-exclusive items you can't buy anywhere else — including a Pikachu wearing round glasses and carrying tropical fruits, inspired by Taiwan being the "Kingdom of Fruit!"

🚇 The Special Pokémon EasyCard

You can buy a Pokémon-themed EasyCard — the card you tap to ride Taipei's MRT subway! It features Pikachu, Jigglypuff, and Dragonite at a Taiwan night market. You can ride the train like a real Pokémon Trainer!

🐉 Meet the Mascots!

Most Pokémon Centers only have Pikachu as a mascot — but Taipei chose Dragonite as a co-mascot too! There's a giant flying Dragonite statue at the entrance. Dragonite was chosen because it looks like it could fly over Taiwan's beautiful island landscape.

🌟 Fun Facts

When it first opened, the crowds were so huge the store needed a ticket system just to get in! A special "Taipei's Pikachu" trading card was given free on opening day — it's now very rare and collectible worldwide. The store also has Ga-Olé arcade machines with space for 140 people!

For curious cousins and grown-ups alike.

Shape of a Banana

Look at Taiwan on a map and you'll see a long, curved island — just like a banana!

Bullet Train

The High Speed Rail zips from Taipei to Kaohsiung in under 2 hours — the whole island, top to bottom!

Mountain Island

About two-thirds of Taiwan is mountains, with more than 200 peaks taller than 3,000 metres.

Sky Lantern Festival

In Pingxi, people write wishes on paper lanterns and let them glow into the night sky.

Butterfly Heaven

Taiwan is home to over 400 species of butterflies — some valleys are nicknamed "Butterfly Valleys".

🎉

Lots of Festivals

Lunar New Year, Lantern Festival, Dragon Boat, Moon Festival — there's almost always something to celebrate.

Shops

Where to shop in Taiwan

From corner-store snacks to towering bookstores, Taiwan has shops everywhere — and most of them stay open late!

🏪

7-Eleven

The convenience king

The most common convenience store in Taiwan — you'll see one on nearly every corner. Pay bills, ship boxes, grab a warm Tea Egg, and cool off with many varieties of ice cream. Open 24 hours, every day!

🍙

FamilyMart

Snacks & sweets

The other big convenience chain (look for the green and blue logo). Famous for fluffy Japanese-style rice balls, soft roll cakes, and exclusive treats from popular Taiwanese brands.

🥐

Bakeries

Soft, sweet, warm

Taiwanese bakeries are famous for fluffy milk bread, taro buns, pineapple buns, and brick-toast cakes. Look for the chain "85°C" — they sell sweet drinks and pastries on almost every street.

📚

Eslite Bookstore

Books, toys, gifts

Eslite (誠品) is a beloved Taiwanese bookstore chain — huge, beautiful, and full of books, stationery, toys, and snacks. Some stores stay open all night!

🛍️

Night Market Stalls

Outdoor shopping

Beyond food, night markets are full of stalls selling toys, plushies, clothes, and gadgets. Try a carnival-style ring-toss or fish-scooping game while you browse!

Coolest Places in Taiwan

Coolest Places in Taiwan ✨

Taiwan is packed with amazing places to explore — from giant shopping cities to iconic landmarks!

🏙️

Taipei 101

One of the most famous buildings in the world — 508 metres tall, shaped like lucky bamboo, with a giant golden ball inside that keeps it safe during typhoons and earthquakes!

🌳

Da'an Forest Park

Taipei's very own Central Park! 26 hectares of trees, ponds, playgrounds, and wildlife right in the middle of the city — and it's completely free to enter, open 24 hours a day!

🏟️

The Big Dome

Taiwan's first and biggest indoor stadium — the Taipei Dome! It holds 40,000 baseball fans and 59,000 concert-goers. Fans waited over 30 years for it, and it finally opened in 2023!

🏬

Shin Kong Mitsukoshi

Taiwan's most famous department store — a giant team-up between Taiwan and Japan! Four huge buildings right next to Taipei 101, connected by sky bridges, packed with food, anime, Pokémon, and so much more.

🏙️ Taipei 101

台北101 — One of the world's most iconic skyscrapers

📏 How Tall is It?

Taipei 101 stands 508 metres tall and has 101 floors above ground (plus 5 underground). From 2004 to 2010 it was the tallest building in the entire world! Today it is still one of the tallest, and the tallest building in Taiwan by far.

🎋 Why Does it Look Like Bamboo?

The building was designed to look like stacked segments of bamboo — a plant that symbolises good luck, strength, and growth in Chinese culture. Each section of 8 floors represents the lucky number 8 (八 bā), which sounds like the word for "prosperity" in Chinese!

🔮 The Giant Golden Ball Inside

One of the coolest things about Taipei 101 is a 660-tonne golden ball hanging between floors 88 and 92 — it's one of the largest of its kind in the world! It's called a tuned mass damper and it swings gently to balance the building during strong winds, typhoons, and earthquakes. You can see it from a special viewing area!

🔭 The Observation Deck

Ride the world's fastest elevator (it was the fastest when built!) to the indoor observation deck on floor 89 in just 37 seconds! On a clear day you can see all of Taipei spread out below. There is also an outdoor deck on floor 91 where you can feel the wind high above the city.

🎆 New Year's Fireworks

Every New Year's Eve, Taipei 101 puts on one of the most spectacular fireworks shows in the world — watched live by hundreds of thousands of people in the streets below and by millions on TV! The fireworks shoot out from different floors of the building all at once.

🛍️ The Mall at the Bottom

The base of the building has a huge luxury shopping mall with restaurants, cafés, designer shops, and a famous food court in the basement. It's a great place to eat after visiting the observation deck!

🚇 How to Get There

Take the MRT Red Line (Tamsui-Xinyi Line) to Taipei 101/World Trade Center Station — the building is right outside Exit 4! The observation deck is open daily from 9:00 AM to 10:00 PM.

🌟 Fun Facts

The number 101 was chosen because it goes one step beyond 100 — the symbol of perfection — meaning always striving to do better! The building has survived major earthquakes and super typhoons. It even has its own Starbucks on the 35th floor — one of the highest Starbucks in the world!

🌳 Da'an Forest Park

大安森林公園 — Taipei's favourite green escape

🏙️ Taipei's Central Park

Da'an Forest Park is often called "the lungs of Taipei" — just like New York City has Central Park, Taipei has this! It covers 26 hectares (about 36 football fields!) right in the heart of the city and is completely free to enter, 24 hours a day.

🛝 What Can Kids Do?

So much! Play on the big colourful adventure playground with slides, swings, and climbing frames. Ride bikes or scoot along the loop paths. Skate at the skating rink. Watch free outdoor concerts at the open-air amphitheatre. Have a picnic on the wide open grass!

🐦 Bird Island!

The park's main pond has a small island in the middle packed with hundreds of birds — locals call it Bird Island! You can spot over 70 species of birds including egrets, kingfishers, herons, and ibises wading in the water. Turtles sunbathe on rocks and black squirrels dash through the trees!

🎵 Concerts and Events

The park has a beautiful open-air amphitheatre that hosts free festivals, music performances, and the famous Taipei Lantern Festival celebrations! Thousands of people gather here for these events — it feels like the whole city comes out to play.

🚇 How to Get There

Super easy! Take the MRT Red Line to Da'an Forest Park Station (大安森林公園站). Exits 2, 3, 4, and 5 all bring you straight into the park. No walking needed — you step out of the train and you're already inside!

🌟 Fun Facts

Before it was a park, this land had illegal buildings on it. The whole area was cleared and turned green — and people called it "Mud Park" while it was being built because it was so messy! Today over 13,000 trees and plants grow here. It was designed so city kids could be in nature without ever leaving Taipei.

🏟️ The Big Dome

台北大巨蛋 — Taiwan's first and biggest indoor stadium

📏 How Big Is It?

The Taipei Dome is 270 metres long and 170 metres wide — about the length of almost 3 football fields side by side! It can hold 40,000 fans for baseball and up to 59,000 people for concerts. It has 36 luxury VIP suites inside. It's the biggest indoor venue in all of Taiwan!

⚾ Baseball — Taiwan's Favourite Sport!

The Dome is home to teams from the CPBL (Chinese Professional Baseball League) — Taiwan's top baseball competition. In 2024 alone, almost 800,000 fans came to watch games here! The very first big event was the 2023 Asian Baseball Championship, where teams from across Asia competed.

🎤 Huge Concerts

Some of Asia's biggest music stars have already performed at the Big Dome — including Jay Chou, one of the most famous pop stars in the Chinese-speaking world! With 59,000 seats, it is one of the biggest concert venues in all of Asia.

🏪 A Whole World Around It

The Dome isn't alone — it's surrounded by a shopping mall, restaurants, a cinema, a hotel, and office buildings. Nearby is also the famous Songshan Cultural Park with art galleries and the iconic Eslite bookstore. You could spend a whole day here!

⛸️ Ice Skating Rink!

Inside the Big Dome complex there is an ice skating rink — one of the very few places in Taiwan where you can skate on real ice! Whether you're a beginner wobbling around the edge or a confident skater spinning in the middle, it's a super fun activity for the whole family. Skate rental is available on site so you don't need to bring your own!

🚇 How to Get There

Take the MRT Blue Line to Sun Yat-Sen Memorial Hall Station (國父紀念館站), Exit 5 — the Dome is just a short walk away. It's in the Xinyi District, close to Taipei 101 and Sun Yat-Sen Memorial Hall.

🌟 The Amazing Story Behind It

Back in 1991, over 13,000 baseball fans sat in the pouring rain watching a championship game. They got so soaked and frustrated they all started chanting "We want a dome! We want a dome!" It took over 30 years to finally build one — but in 2023, it was done! The roof never opens, so games are never rained out ever again. ☔➡️🙅

🏬 Shin Kong Mitsukoshi

新光三越 — Taiwan's most famous department store

🤝 Taiwan Meets Japan

Mitsukoshi started in Japan in 1673 — over 350 years ago! It began as a tiny fabric shop in old Tokyo. In 1991 it joined with Taiwanese company Shin Kong to open stores in Taiwan. Today you'll find Japanese food, Japanese anime shops, and Taiwanese favourites all under one roof!

🏙️ Four Buildings in One Place

In the Xinyi District right next to Taipei 101, there are four buildings — A4, A8, A9, and A11! They're connected by sky bridges so you can walk between them without going outside. Together they make one giant shopping city!

🐱 The Totoro Cat Bus (A8)

Inside the A8 building is a Studio Ghibli store with a life-size Totoro Cat Bus that kids can actually go inside and take photos! If you love My Neighbour Totoro or Spirited Away, this is a must-visit spot.

🎮 Pokémon Center (A11)

The A11 building is home to Taiwan's official Pokémon Center — only the second one ever opened outside Japan in the whole world! Find it on the 3rd floor, packed with exclusive Taiwan-only Pokémon merchandise.

🥟 Amazing Food Halls

Head to the basement levels for a food lover's paradise! Giant markets sell Taiwanese snacks, Japanese sweets, fresh cakes, and famous restaurants like Din Tai Fung — home of Taiwan's world-famous soup dumplings (小籠包 xiǎo lóng bāo).

🚇 Easy to Get To

Take the MRT Blue Line to Taipei City Hall Station — the A8 building is right at the exit! Or take the Red Line to Taipei 101/World Trade Center Station and walk a few minutes north. Open every day until at least 9:30 PM.

🌟 Fun Facts

Over 120 million people visit Shin Kong Mitsukoshi stores across Taiwan every year — that's more than 3 times Taiwan's entire population! During big anniversary sales, one store made over NT$2 billion in a single day!

Safety

Staying safe in Taiwan

Taiwan is a friendly and safe place, but two big natural events are good to know about. Don't worry — locals are very prepared!

📳

Earthquakes

地震 · Shaking ground

Taiwan sits on the Ring of Fire, so the ground shakes here pretty often. The most important thing is to stay calm — Taiwan's buildings are designed to handle earthquakes really well. Phones blast a loud alarm a few seconds before a big quake!

🌀

Typhoons

颱風 · Big storms

Hurricanes are called typhoons here. They mostly come between June and October with strong winds and heavy rain. When one arrives, schools and offices close, and everyone stays cosy inside until it passes — usually a day or two.

🚨

Emergency Numbers

Good to know

Dial 110 for police, 119 for fire or ambulance, and 112 from any mobile phone if you're lost or in trouble. Taiwan also offers help in English.
